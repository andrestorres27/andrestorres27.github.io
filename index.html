Pipeline Analysis Tool

CourseMECH 45 Personal Project
SoftwareMATLAB
TimelineNov. 2025 – Dec. 2025
StatusComplete

A MATLAB-based tool that evaluates the structural integrity, hydraulic performance, and potential failure modes of a pressurized PVC pipeline. It applies solid mechanics, fluid dynamics, and mechanical design to show how operating pressure, pipe geometry, loading, and support spacing affect reliability.

Four integrated analyses

  1. Pressure and bending stress: hoop stress, bending stress, combined stress, and factor of safety under internal pressure and uniformly distributed loading.
  2. Hydraulic flow: Reynolds number and the Darcy-Weisbach equation to characterize flow, friction factors, and pressure losses.
  3. Pipe support optimization: allowable support spacing from bending stress and beam deflection constraints.
  4. Failure mode and risk assessment: joint separation forces, excessive deflection, and wall-thickness reduction, using sensitivity analysis and a risk-ranking matrix.

Results

A nominal 6-inch Schedule 40 PVC pipeline was modeled at 150 psi internal pressure with an initial support spacing of 2.0 m. The program calculated a combined stress of 12.04 MPa, turbulent flow (Re = 8.25 × 10⁴), and a maximum allowable support spacing of approximately 1.93 m. Combined stress exceeds the 12 MPa allowable at about 1.96 m of spacing, deflection rises rapidly as spacing grows, and reduced wall thickness raises hoop stress and lowers the factor of safety.

Leatherman Multi Tool

CourseMECH 115 Junior Design Project
SoftwareOnshape, SolidWorks
TimelineMar. 2026 – Jun. 2026
StatusComplete

Team design and analysis of a Leatherman Free P4-inspired multi tool with several folding attachments in a compact assembly. The project combined preliminary CAD (Onshape), assembly motion studies (SolidWorks), and finite element analysis (SolidWorks) to evaluate geometry, rotation, structural loading, and design improvements.

My contributions

  • Preliminary CAD modeling: Supported the early modeling of component geometries and assembly layouts, accounting for the spacing and rotational clearance required by the folding tools.
  • Finite element analysis: Assisted with static structural simulations of the plier and handle assembly to examine stress concentrations and deformation under applied loading.
  • Motion study: Simulated the multi tool folding mechanism to evaluate rotation about pin joints, tool deployment, and assembly functionality.

Results

The team's static FEA of the AISI 304 stainless steel assembly used an approximately 1,000 N load. It identified the connecting pins as critical stress regions and the handle ends as the area of greatest displacement, which informed changes to pin dimensions and assembly geometry.

Keycap and Switch Fit

CourseMECH 171 GD&T Exhibition
SoftwareSolidWorks
TimelineNov. 2026 – Present
StatusPreliminary CAD complete; analysis in progress

Modeled a simplified ESC keyboard keycap and mating switch interface in SolidWorks to examine how the cross-shaped connection influences component fit, alignment, and potential manufacturing variation. The current work establishes preliminary component geometry and an assembly representation; final dimensions and tolerances are not yet specified.

Completed CAD development

  • Component modeling: Developed keycap geometry, the cross-shaped mating socket, and a simplified switch/stem representation.
  • Assembly development: Positioned the modeled components to illustrate their intended engagement and alignment.
  • Geometry inspection: Used exploded and transparent views to examine the internal connection and its mating features.

Preliminary CAD views

Next steps: Create dimensioned engineering drawings, select datum reference frames, apply GD&T controls in accordance with ASME Y14.5, and conduct tolerance stack-up and exact-constraint assessments. No functional fit or production tolerances have been validated yet.

Regenerative Shock Absorber

CourseSenior MECH Design
SoftwareSolidWorks
TimelineOct. 2026 – Present
StatusIn progress

A regenerative shock absorber for electric vehicles that converts suspension damping motion into electrical power. The selected architecture couples a rack-and-pinion transmission and DC generator with a switched resistive load and battery-charging system. The aim is to recover dissipated energy while preserving conventional damping performance and practical EV integration.

Objectives

  1. Mechanical design and integration: a rack-and-pinion assembly that converts linear suspension travel into generator shaft rotation.
  2. Electrical power generation: a DC generator integrated with the transmission, targeting 5–10 W during a five-minute controlled test.
  3. Power management and storage: a switched resistive load for repeatable power measurement and a battery-charging path to store recovered energy.
  4. Prototype testing and validation: compare damping behavior, power output, recovered energy, and physical constraints against a conventional shock absorber.
Current progress: Preliminary SolidWorks modeling of the hydraulic chamber housing and separate rack-and-pinion transmission concept is complete. Detailed integration, fabrication, and bench testing are upcoming; energy-recovery and damping targets remain unvalidated.

Concept of operation and requirements

The concept converts suspension motion into useful electrical output, alongside the mechanical and thermal effects of damping. The table lists benchmark constraints for an EV-compatible prototype.

Trade-off studies

Mechanical subsystem. Five transmission mechanisms were weighted on damping consistency (30%), energy recovery (25%), assembly (20%), maintenance (15%), and cost (10%). Rack-and-pinion scored highest (+55), followed by opposed cables (+50) and timing belt (+45).

Electrical subsystem. Five generator-loading and energy-management configurations were weighted on energy storage (30%), testing repeatability (25%), manufacturing and assembly (25%), damping (10%), and cost (10%). The switch-load and battery-charging system ranked first (+55), followed by rectifier with resistive load (+40).

Preliminary CAD — Hydraulic Chamber Housing

Developed a preliminary cylindrical shock absorber housing assembly in SolidWorks to establish outer geometry, end-connection features, and the spatial arrangement of internal components. Transparent assembly views illustrate the intended chamber layout; sealing, fluid behavior, and structural performance have not yet been assessed experimentally.

Preliminary CAD — Rack-and-Pinion Transmission

Modeled a separate rack-and-pinion transmission concept with a gear shaft and bevel-gear arrangement intended to translate linear suspension travel into rotary motion for a generator. These views establish conceptual mating geometry; the mechanism has not yet been fully integrated with the hydraulic housing or validated for motion, loading, or power output.
